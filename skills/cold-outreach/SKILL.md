# Cold Outreach That Isn't a Robot

Write a cold email a busy person would actually reply to. This only works when there is a true, specific reason for the outreach, so get that first.

## Intake

Ask for three things, then write. Do not ask the sender to draft a line.

- The recipient's title and company.
- **The real, specific thing you noticed about them** (a launch, a hire, a post, a job opening, a change). This is the whole email.
- The one outcome you help with, in plain words.

If you are given a link (their post, their job listing, their launch) and you can fetch pages, read it and pull the specific detail yourself. If there is nothing true and specific to open with, say so and stop. Do not manufacture fake familiarity; that is the one input the skill cannot invent.

## Rules for the email

- Under 90 words.
- Open with them, not with you or your company.
- One specific observation. One low-friction question, not a meeting demand.
- Banned: "hope this finds you well," "quick call," "circle back," "touch base," buzzwords, and any fake "I loved your post" familiarity.
- No em dashes.

## Self-check before you hand it back

- **Delete-the-first-line test:** if the opening line would work in an email to anyone else, it is not specific enough. Rewrite it around the one true detail.
- **Length test:** count the words. Over 90, cut.
- **Reply test:** read the ask as the recipient. If it demands a meeting or a decision, soften it to a single easy question they can answer in one line.
- **Voice:** zero em dashes, zero banned phrases above. Run the pack's `anti-robot-editor` scanner on the draft if you have a shell; fix anything it flags.

Revise until it passes, then return two versions.

## Output

Two versions, each with its own short subject line (under 6 words, lowercase-optional, no clickbait). Note in one line what is different about each so the sender can pick.

## Example

Detail noticed: the recipient (a VP of Support at a 300-person SaaS) just posted that their team's ticket volume doubled after a pricing change.

Version A, subject "ticket spike after pricing": "Saw your note about tickets doubling since the pricing change. The pattern we see: it is usually three or four repeat questions driving most of the spike, not broad confusion. Worth me sending the two we see most often for a setup like yours? No call needed, just a reply." Version B leads on the number (opens with "Doubled ticket volume") and asks whether they have mapped the top drivers yet. A is softer, B is more direct.
